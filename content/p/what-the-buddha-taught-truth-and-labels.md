Ideas of Buddhism have always been intriguing.

I have no idea if the Buddha really existed, people seem to be highly confident
he did. But to me, that is of less importance. When you are reading an
understanding a doctrine, does it really matter who said it?

## Truth and labels

> "Is Buddhism a religion or a philisophy?"

Truth has no labels, Rahula says. Rightly so.

It does not matter if a treatment is "Ayurvedic" or "Allopahtic", for if the
treatment _works_, it remains true that it works, regardless of the label.

This one is close to home to me. I have always thought of truth this way: if a
true statement comes out of a book or religion I heavily disagree with, I want
to be able to accept it as true without prejudice.

Labeling does more diservice than good. It brings all the prejudice that comes
with the label. Rahula says, when you label a man English, German or any other
nationality, you see him with the prejudice that comes with it, with things
people attribute to that nationality, which the person you are labeling might
not even have.

And this is also why I refuse to identify myself with a religion.

## Mahavira

I did not know (or realise) that Mahavira and Buddha lived in the same era. The
book says Mahavira was probably a few years older than Buddha.

It talks about how once Mahavira sent his disciple to Nalanda in order to
debate with Buddha about Krama, as they both disagreed on things around it.
From the debate, the deciple was convinced that his teacher was wrong and
wanted Buddha to accept him as his student.

I have little idea about what Mahavira taught. Perhaps that is something I want
to learn once I am done reading this one.

I am interested specifially now in how both these people understood and taught
Karma.
